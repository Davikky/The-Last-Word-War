# Purpose

Playtest #2 is the first structured evaluation conducted after the stabilization of the project's gameplay philosophy, software architecture, and constitutional documentation.

Unlike earlier internal testing, the purpose of this playtest is not to design new gameplay mechanics.

Instead, the objective is to evaluate whether the implemented software successfully delivers the approved gameplay experience to real players.

The playtest therefore serves three complementary purposes:

- validate software implementation;
- evaluate player experience;
- collect research observations.

The results will guide the completion of the Architecture Stabilization Phase.

---

# Scope

Playtest #2 evaluates the complete multiplayer gameplay experience from room creation through official match completion.

The playtest includes evaluation of:

- onboarding;
- multiplayer room management;
- gameplay flow;
- user experience;
- synchronization reliability;
- match completion;
- player understanding;
- post-match feedback.

The playtest does not introduce experimental gameplay mechanics.

Only approved gameplay rules shall be evaluated.

---

# Guiding Principle

The playtest evaluates implementation rather than design.

The approved specifications contained within:

- Rulebook v2.0;
- Gameplay Philosophy;
- Game Engine Specification;
- Match Completion Rules;
- Victory Conditions;

remain authoritative throughout testing.

Observed implementation issues should be recorded for future correction rather than resolved during active playtesting.

---

# Primary Objectives

Playtest #2 seeks to answer the following questions.

## Objective 1

Can first-time players understand the game without external instruction?

---

## Objective 2

Does multiplayer gameplay proceed smoothly from room creation to official match completion?

---

## Objective 3

Do players understand the relationship between:

- current word;
- turn order;
- score progression;
- timeout penalties;
- Final Round;
- Sudden Death?

---

## Objective 4

Does the software consistently implement the approved gameplay specification?

---

## Objective 5

Does the gameplay encourage meaningful Human ↔ Human interaction consistent with the project's philosophy?

# Participants

The objective of Playtest #2 is to observe authentic first-time interaction with The Last Word War™.

Participants should therefore have little or no prior exposure to the game.

## Recommended Number of Players

Minimum:

**2**

Recommended:

**3–4**

Maximum:

Determined by the current software implementation.

Larger groups may be evaluated during future playtesting phases.

---

# Participant Profile

Preferred participants should:

- have no previous experience with The Last Word War™;
- possess basic computer literacy;
- be capable of participating in online multiplayer gameplay;
- communicate naturally without attempting to "test the software."

Players should experience the game as ordinary users rather than software testers.

---

# Observer Role

Observers facilitate the session without directing gameplay.

Their primary responsibility is to record observations rather than influence player behaviour.

Observers should:

- introduce the purpose of the session;
- explain only the minimum information necessary to begin;
- avoid teaching gameplay strategies;
- avoid correcting player mistakes unless gameplay cannot continue;
- record observations objectively.

The observer should never coach participants toward the "correct" behaviour during active gameplay.

---

# Player Responsibilities

Participants should be encouraged to interact with the software naturally.

Players should:

- create or join multiplayer rooms;
- read the landing page;
- explore the interface independently;
- discuss gameplay naturally with one another;
- report confusion when it occurs;
- complete the match whenever possible;
- provide honest post-match feedback.

Questions should be answered only when necessary to allow gameplay to continue.

---

# Test Environment

The playtest should closely resemble a normal multiplayer session.

Whenever possible:

- each participant should use their own device;
- participants should connect independently;
- normal internet connectivity should be used;
- no developer tools should be visible.

The objective is to observe realistic gameplay conditions rather than laboratory-style testing.

---

# Test Scenarios

Playtest #2 should evaluate the complete player journey.

## Scenario 1 — Initial Discovery

Players should attempt to understand the game using only:

- the landing page;
- the Quick Rules;
- the interface.

Observe:

- initial reactions;
- questions;
- assumptions;
- misconceptions.

---

## Scenario 2 — Room Creation

Evaluate whether players can successfully:

- create a room;
- share the room code;
- join the room;
- identify the room host;
- begin the match.

Observe any hesitation or confusion.

---

## Scenario 3 — Core Gameplay

Observe whether players naturally understand:

- whose turn it is;
- the Current Word;
- sentence submission;
- score updates;
- timeout behaviour.

Pay particular attention to moments where players hesitate before taking action.

---

## Scenario 4 — Match Completion

Observe player understanding of:

- Final Round;
- Sudden Death (if applicable);
- winner determination;
- draw outcomes (if encountered);
- inactivity handling (if encountered).

Players should be able to understand why the match ended.

---

## Scenario 5 — Rematch

If appropriate, conduct a second match.

Observe whether players can:

- initiate a rematch;
- understand the new starting player;
- continue gameplay without additional instruction.

---

## Research Observations

Particular attention should be given to evidence supporting or challenging the project's central philosophy.

Questions include:

- Does the gameplay encourage meaningful Human ↔ Human interaction?
- Do players naturally negotiate meaning and interpretation?
- Does the software successfully facilitate rather than dominate interaction?
- Are disagreements resolved socially rather than computationally?

These observations contribute directly to the project's ongoing research programme.

# Success Criteria

Playtest #2 shall be considered successful if the software consistently demonstrates the approved gameplay behaviour while providing an enjoyable and understandable experience for first-time players.

Success is evaluated across three complementary dimensions.

---

## Gameplay Success

The playtest shall demonstrate that:

- players can create and join multiplayer rooms successfully;
- players understand the Current Word mechanic;
- turn order remains clear throughout the match;
- score progression is easily understood;
- timeout penalties behave as expected;
- Final Round operates correctly;
- Sudden Death behaves correctly when applicable;
- official match completion is clearly understood by all participants.

---

## Software Success

The software shall demonstrate:

- stable multiplayer synchronization;
- consistent countdown behaviour;
- reliable room state synchronization;
- successful browser session recovery (where applicable);
- absence of critical gameplay defects.

No Critical engineering issues should be observed during normal gameplay.

---

## User Experience Success

Players should demonstrate that they can:

- understand the basic gameplay with minimal assistance;
- navigate the interface naturally;
- complete an entire multiplayer match;
- understand why the match concluded;
- express willingness to participate again.

Confusion should decrease naturally as gameplay progresses.

---

# Observation Recording Guidelines

Observers should distinguish between four different categories of observations.

## Usability

Examples include:

- confusing interface layouts;
- unclear terminology;
- hidden functionality;
- unnecessary player hesitation.

---

## Gameplay

Examples include:

- misunderstandings of the sentence rule;
- uncertainty regarding turn order;
- difficulty understanding Final Round;
- confusion surrounding Sudden Death.

---

## Technical

Examples include:

- synchronization delays;
- timeout inconsistencies;
- browser compatibility issues;
- multiplayer state errors;
- unexpected software behaviour.

Technical observations should later be cross-referenced with the Engineering Issue Register.

---

## Research

Examples include:

- spontaneous player interaction;
- negotiation of sentence interpretation;
- collaborative problem solving;
- competitive behaviour;
- evidence supporting Human ↔ Human interaction.

Research observations should be recorded objectively without attempting to influence player behaviour.

---

# Post-Test Activities

Following completion of every playtest session, the following activities should be performed.

## Gameplay Review

Review the complete gameplay session with participating players.

Discuss:

- confusing moments;
- enjoyable moments;
- unexpected behaviour;
- suggested improvements.

---

## Engineering Review

Review all technical observations.

Determine whether each observation represents:

- a software defect;
- an implementation gap;
- technical debt;
- an enhancement opportunity.

Update the Engineering Issue Register where appropriate.

---

## Documentation Review

Update the following documents as necessary:

- Engineering Issue Register;
- Development Journal;
- Playtest Notes;
- Release Notes;
- Project State (if significant changes occurred).

Documentation should remain synchronized with implementation throughout the Architecture Stabilization Phase.
