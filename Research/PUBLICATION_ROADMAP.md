# Purpose

This document defines the long-term publication strategy for **The Last Word War™**.

The publication programme exists to communicate the scientific, engineering, and educational contributions emerging from the project.

Unlike software documentation, which describes implementation, publications communicate new knowledge produced throughout the project's development.

The publication programme therefore seeks to:

- preserve intellectual history;
- establish scholarly precedence;
- communicate original research contributions;
- encourage reproducibility;
- support future collaborations.

Publications shall prioritize evidence, transparency, reproducibility, and scholarly integrity.

---

# Research Vision

The Last Word War™ is both a multiplayer software platform and a long-term research programme.

The project explores how carefully designed computational systems can strengthen Human ↔ Human interaction without replacing human judgment.

Rather than positioning artificial intelligence as the primary participant, the project investigates how computation can facilitate meaningful interaction between people.

This philosophy is captured by the principle of **Computational Facilitation**, which has become the central conceptual framework guiding both software development and future research.

---

# Research Philosophy

Research within The Last Word War™ follows several guiding principles.

## Human-Centered Computing

People remain the primary creators of meaning, interpretation, and social interaction.

---

## Computational Facilitation

Computing exists to facilitate interaction rather than govern it.

The software provides the computational framework within which human communication occurs.

---

## Evidence-Driven Development

Research conclusions shall be supported by:

- engineering validation;
- structured playtesting;
- player feedback;
- documented observations.

Assertions should be based upon reproducible evidence rather than design intentions alone.

---

## Transparent Development

The project's documentation preserves the complete evolution of:

- ideas;
- software architecture;
- implementation;
- validation;
- research.

This transparency supports reproducibility and scholarly credibility.

# Evidence Collection Strategy

The quality of every publication depends upon the quality of the supporting evidence.

The Last Word War™ therefore adopts an evidence-driven publication strategy in which research conclusions emerge from documented engineering practice, structured validation, and observed player behaviour.

Evidence should be collected continuously throughout the lifetime of the project rather than immediately prior to publication.

---

Evidence may include:

- sentence histories;

- match completion data;
- player behaviour;
- interaction patterns.

Where appropriate, gameplay evidence should be anonymized before research use.

# Open Research Questions

Although significant progress has been made, several research questions remain open.

Examples include:

- How effectively does Computational Facilitation encourage meaningful Human ↔ Human interaction?
- Does sentence-chain gameplay improve conversational continuity?
- How does gameplay behaviour change with increasing player experience?
- Can the framework be adapted successfully to educational environments?
- What role should artificial intelligence play outside gameplay while preserving human agency?

Future publications should seek to answer these questions using empirical evidence rather than theoretical speculation.

# Long-Term Vision

The long-term ambition of The Last Word War™ extends beyond the creation of a multiplayer game.

The project seeks to demonstrate that carefully designed computational systems can strengthen human communication while deliberately preserving human judgment, creativity, and social responsibility.

Through a combination of transparent software engineering, disciplined documentation, structured validation, and evidence-driven research, the project aims to contribute a practical and reproducible model for designing human-centered digital systems.

The resulting publications are intended not only to document the evolution of The Last Word War™, but also to contribute broadly to discussions in human-centered computing, software engineering, educational technology, and the responsible integration of computational systems into human activities.
