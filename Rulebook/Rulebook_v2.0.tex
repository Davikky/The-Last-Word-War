\section{Welcome}

Welcome to \textbf{The Last Word War\texttrademark}.

The Last Word War\texttrademark\ transforms ordinary conversation into a shared challenge of language, creativity, reasoning, and human interaction.

Every sentence begins with the final word of the previous player's sentence, creating an evolving chain of ideas shaped entirely by the participants themselves.

The objective is not merely to score points.

The objective is to communicate, think, learn, adapt, and enjoy meaningful interaction with other people.

Unlike many digital games, The Last Word War\texttrademark\ intentionally preserves human judgment throughout gameplay.

The software coordinates the match.

The players determine its meaning.

% =========================================================
\section{Purpose of this Rulebook}

This Rulebook defines the official rules governing gameplay in The Last Word War\texttrademark.

It explains:

\begin{itemize}
    \item how to prepare a match;
    \item how turns are played;
    \item how points are earned;
    \item how matches are completed;
    \item how winners are determined;
    \item the responsibilities of both the software and the players.
\end{itemize}

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, these rules apply equally to casual games, classroom activities, online multiplayer matches, tournaments, and future official editions.

\section{Gameplay Philosophy}

The Last Word War\texttrademark\ is fundamentally a human-to-human interaction game.

Its purpose is not to produce perfect sentences, automate every judgment, or eliminate every ambiguity. Its purpose is to create a shared environment in which people communicate, think, learn, compete, negotiate, and build ideas together.

\begin{quote}
The software provides the framework of play.

The players define the culture of play.
\end{quote}

The software coordinates objective gameplay functions.

Human participants remain responsible for meaning, interpretation, creativity, honesty, negotiation, and social responsibility.

\subsection{Human-Governed Gameplay}

Gameplay is intentionally governed by the participating players.

Players collectively determine how they respond to:

\begin{itemize}
    \item ambiguous sentence construction;
    \item unusual interpretations;
    \item creative uses of language;
    \item questionable continuity;
    \item playful use of rule boundaries;
    \item disagreements over meaning;
    \item locally agreed house rules.
\end{itemize}

The game does not require every disagreement to be resolved by software.

Players may discuss, challenge, accept, reject, or negotiate a disputed contribution according to the culture established for that match.

Disagreement is not necessarily a failure of gameplay.

It may form part of the interaction itself.

\subsection{Computational Facilitation}

The Last Word War\texttrademark\ adopts the principle of \textbf{Computational Facilitation}.

Computing exists to coordinate interaction rather than govern its meaning.

The software may provide:

\begin{itemize}
    \item room creation and joining;
    \item multiplayer synchronization;
    \item turn order;
    \item countdown timing;
    \item score tracking;
    \item sentence history;
    \item automatic last-word extraction;
    \item session recovery;
    \item multiplayer integrity.
\end{itemize}

The software intentionally does not:

\begin{itemize}
    \item judge creativity;
    \item determine sentence quality;
    \item interpret player intent;
    \item resolve disputes;
    \item enforce local house rules;
    \item define acceptable gameplay culture.
\end{itemize}

The system facilitates interaction.

Humans create meaning.

\subsection{Interpretive Freedom}

Language is flexible and context-dependent.

Meaning may be influenced by:

\begin{itemize}
    \item culture;
    \item humour;
    \item intention;
    \item experience;
    \item imagination;
    \item local usage.
\end{itemize}

The game intentionally preserves this interpretive freedom.

Players may discover unconventional strategies or develop local conventions that are not fully anticipated by the software.

Such situations should ordinarily be addressed through communication between the participants rather than compulsory automated judgment.

\subsection{Time as the Fundamental Constraint}

Time is the primary objective gameplay constraint.

Each player receives a limited opportunity to act during their turn.

The countdown introduces:

\begin{itemize}
    \item urgency;
    \item rhythm;
    \item anticipation;
    \item consequence;
    \item pressure;
    \item emotional investment.
\end{itemize}

Unlike meaning or creativity, time can be coordinated objectively by the software.

Timeout is therefore not merely a technical feature.

It is one of the central foundations of gameplay.

\subsection{Gameplay Over Perfection}

The Last Word War\texttrademark\ does not seek algorithmic perfection.

The software should be reliable and predictable, but gameplay should remain human.

The game intentionally preserves opportunities for:

\begin{itemize}
    \item creativity;
    \item trust;
    \item negotiation;
    \item disagreement;
    \item strategic thinking;
    \item playful experimentation;
    \item shared learning.
\end{itemize}

The objective is not to remove every loophole or automate every judgment.

The objective is to preserve meaningful human interaction.

\subsection{Scoreboard}

The scoreboard records each player's official score throughout the match.

Scores remain visible to all participants.

\subsection{Sentence History}

Every accepted sentence becomes part of the official match history.

The sentence history serves as the official record of gameplay and allows players to review the evolving chain of ideas throughout the match.

\subsection{Current Word}

The current word is the final word extracted from the previous accepted sentence.

Each new sentence must begin with this word.

The software automatically extracts and displays the current word during digital gameplay.

\section{Match Setup}

Before gameplay begins, the following steps are completed.

\subsection{Step 1 --- Create or Join a Match}

One player creates a new room.

Other players join the room before gameplay begins.

Each participant chooses the display name that will appear throughout the match.

\subsection{Step 2 --- Verify Participants}

Before starting the match, all participants should confirm that:

\begin{itemize}
    \item every intended player has joined;
    \item player names are displayed correctly;
    \item everyone understands the selected game mode.
\end{itemize}

Once gameplay begins, no additional players may join the current match.

Any additional participant must wait until a new match is created.

Depending on the selected edition or platform, players may configure:

\begin{itemize}
    \item game mode;
    \item turn duration;
    \item target score (where supported);

Unless explicitly changed, the official default target score is:

\end{center}

\subsection{Step 4 --- Determine the Starting Player}

The first player is determined according to the selected match settings.

For rematches, the starting player rotates automatically in accordance with the official Starter Rotation rules described later in this Rulebook.

When all participants are ready, the host starts the match.

Once gameplay begins:

\begin{itemize}
    \item player registration closes;
    \item the first turn begins;
    \item the countdown timer starts;
    \item the match officially enters Active Play.
\end{itemize}

From this point onward, gameplay proceeds according to the official rules described in the following sections.

\section{Core Gameplay}

The Last Word War\texttrademark\ is played as a sequence of turns in which players collaboratively construct an evolving chain of sentences.

Each new sentence must begin with the final word of the previous accepted sentence.

Gameplay continues until the official match completion conditions described later in this Rulebook are satisfied.

% =========================================================
\section{The Objective}

The objective of the game is to accumulate the highest score while participating in a continuous chain of meaningful interaction.

Success depends upon a player's ability to:

\begin{itemize}
    \item respond quickly;
    \item adapt to changing words;
    \item construct appropriate sentences;
    \item maintain continuity where possible;
    \item think creatively under time pressure.
\end{itemize}

Although players compete for points, the game is fundamentally designed to encourage communication, learning, creativity, and enjoyable human interaction.

% =========================================================
\section{Turn Structure}

Gameplay proceeds one player at a time.

Each turn follows the same sequence.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The current player receives the active word.
    \item The countdown timer begins.
    \item The player constructs a sentence beginning with the active word.
    \item The sentence is submitted before the timer expires.
    \item The software extracts the final word.
    \item Scores are updated where appropriate.
    \item The next player receives the newly extracted word.
\end{enumerate}

This sequence repeats until the match officially concludes.

% =========================================================
\section{Beginning a Sentence}

Every accepted sentence must begin with the current word displayed by the game.

Example:

\begin{quote}
Current Word:

\textbf{Knowledge}
\end{quote}

Acceptable opening:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Knowledge} enables people to solve difficult problems.
\end{quote}

The software automatically determines the next current word by extracting the final word of the accepted sentence.

% =========================================================
\section{Ending a Turn}

A player's turn ends immediately when one of the following occurs.

\subsection*{Successful Submission}

The player submits a sentence before the countdown expires.

The software:

\begin{itemize}
    \item records the sentence;
    \item extracts the final word;
    \item updates the match history;
    \item applies any score changes;
    \item resets that player's consecutive timeout counter;
    \item advances play to the next participant.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Timeout}

If the player fails to submit a sentence before the countdown reaches zero:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the turn immediately ends;
    \item a timeout penalty is recorded;
    \item the player's consecutive timeout counter increases by one;
    \item play advances according to the official timeout rules.
\end{itemize}

Timeouts are explained fully in Section~\ref{sec:timeouts}.

% =========================================================
\section{Sentence History}

Every accepted sentence becomes part of the official match history.

The sentence history serves as the permanent record of the completed match and allows players to review the evolution of the conversation from beginning to end.

Once accepted, previous sentences remain part of the official record even if the player later becomes inactive or is removed from the match.

% =========================================================
\section{Current Word Progression}

At any moment during gameplay, only one current word exists.

The progression follows the sequence:

\begin{center}
Current Word

$\downarrow$

Player Submission

$\downarrow$

Automatic Last-Word Extraction

$\downarrow$

New Current Word

$\downarrow$

Next Player
\end{center}

This process repeats continuously throughout Active Play, the Final Round, and Sudden Death.

% =========================================================
\section{Scoring System}

The scoring system rewards successful participation while preserving the human-centered nature of gameplay.

Unless a game mode specifies otherwise, the following rules apply.

\subsection{Successful Turn}

A player earns one point for every successfully accepted turn completed before the countdown timer expires.

A successful turn requires:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the sentence to begin with the current word;
    \item submission before timeout;
    \item successful acceptance into the official match history.
\end{itemize}

Once accepted:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the player's score increases;
    \item the sentence is permanently recorded;
    \item the final word becomes the next current word;
    \item the player's consecutive timeout counter is reset to zero.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Timeout}

A player who fails to submit before the countdown expires receives no point for that turn.

Instead:

\begin{itemize}
    \item a timeout penalty is recorded;
    \item the consecutive timeout counter increases by one;
    \item play immediately advances to the next player.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Official Scoreboard}

The scoreboard always represents the official score of the match.

Scores displayed during gameplay remain the only official scores used to determine the outcome of the match.

No post-game review or analysis may alter the official scoreboard.

% =========================================================
\section{Official Match States}

Every official match progresses through a defined sequence of gameplay states.

\subsection{Setup}

Players join the room, configure the match, and prepare for gameplay.

During this stage:

\begin{itemize}
    \item players may join;
    \item settings may be configured;
    \item the starting player is determined.
\end{itemize}

The match has not yet officially begun.

\subsection{Active Play}

Gameplay proceeds normally.

Players take turns according to the established turn order while accumulating points.

The match remains in Active Play until a player reaches the target score.

\subsection{Final Round}

When any player reaches the target score, the match immediately enters the Final Round.

The match does not end immediately.

Every remaining active player must still receive one final turn before the winner is determined.

This guarantees equal turn opportunities for every participant.

\subsection{Sudden Death}

If two or more players remain tied for the highest score after the Final Round, only those tied players continue.

Sudden Death continues until either:

\begin{itemize}
    \item one player leads by at least two points; or
    \item the official Sudden Death draw limit is reached.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Match Complete}

A match enters the Match Complete state when:

\begin{itemize}
    \item a winner has been determined;
    \item a draw has been declared;
    \item the match has been abandoned through inactivity;
    \item exceptional match completion has occurred.
\end{itemize}

Once the match is complete:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the official scoreboard becomes final;
    \item the sentence history becomes permanent;
    \item optional AI Gameplay Analysis may be requested.
\end{itemize}

\section{Multiplayer Integrity}
\label{sec:multiplayer-integrity}

The Last Word War\texttrademark\ preserves fair and continuous multiplayer gameplay through a small number of objective computational rules.

These rules coordinate gameplay only.

They do not govern interpretation or creativity.

\subsection{Closed Match}

Once Active Play begins:

\begin{itemize}
    \item no additional players may join the current match;
    \item all registered participants continue until the match concludes or are removed due to inactivity.
\end{itemize}

Any new participant must wait until a new match is created.

\subsection{Player Identity}

Each participant retains a single identity throughout the match.

Player names, scores, and previously accepted sentences remain part of the official match record.

\subsection{Automatic Last-Word Extraction}

After every accepted sentence, the software automatically extracts the final word.

This extracted word becomes the required starting word for the next player's turn.

The extraction process is objective and identical for every player.

\subsection{Shared Match History}

All participants observe the same official match history.

The history includes:

\begin{itemize}
    \item accepted sentences;
    \item player names;
    \item score progression;
    \item official match outcome.
\end{itemize}

The shared history serves as the permanent record of the completed match.

\subsection{Computational Coordination}

Throughout gameplay, the software coordinates:

\begin{itemize}
    \item multiplayer synchronization;
    \item turn order;
    \item countdown timing;
    \item score tracking;
    \item sentence history;
    \item session continuity;
    \item multiplayer integrity.
\end{itemize}

Interpretation, creativity, negotiation, and gameplay culture remain entirely the responsibility of the players.

% =========================================================
\section{Timeouts}
\label{sec:timeouts}

Time is one of the defining characteristics of The Last Word War\texttrademark.

Every player receives an equal opportunity to contribute within a limited period.

The countdown timer maintains the pace of the match and ensures fairness among all participants.

\subsection{Successful Turn}

A turn is considered successful when the player submits an accepted sentence before the countdown timer expires.

A successful turn immediately:

\begin{itemize}
    \item records the sentence;
    \item updates the official match history;
    \item awards the appropriate score;
    \item extracts the next current word;
    \item resets that player's consecutive timeout counter to zero;
    \item advances play to the next participant.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Timeout}

A timeout occurs when a player fails to submit an accepted sentence before the countdown reaches zero.

When a timeout occurs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item no point is awarded;
    \item the player's consecutive timeout counter increases by one;
    \item play immediately advances to the next player.
\end{itemize}

Timeouts are objective computational events and require no human interpretation.

% =========================================================
\section{Consecutive Timeout Rule}

The game distinguishes between an occasional missed turn and sustained inactivity.

Only consecutive timeout penalties contribute toward automatic removal.

\subsection{Resetting the Counter}

Whenever a player successfully completes a turn, that player's consecutive timeout counter is immediately reset to:

\[
0
\]

Previous timeout penalties no longer contribute toward automatic removal.

Example:

\begin{center}
Timeout $\rightarrow$ Timeout $\rightarrow$ Successful Turn $\rightarrow$ Timeout
\end{center}

The final timeout shown above is treated as the player's first consecutive timeout.

% =========================================================
\section{Automatic Removal for Inactivity}

To preserve multiplayer continuity, a player who accumulates:

\begin{center}
\Large\textbf{Three Consecutive Timeout Penalties}
\end{center}

is automatically removed from the current match.

Automatic removal is performed entirely by the software and requires no player vote.

Upon removal:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the player's existing score is preserved;
    \item all previously accepted sentences remain in the official match history;
    \item the player takes no further turns during the current match;
    \item turn order is automatically recalculated using the remaining active players.
\end{itemize}

Automatic removal exists solely to preserve the continuity of multiplayer gameplay.

It is not intended as a punishment.

% =========================================================
\section{Examples}

\subsection*{Example 1}

Player A times out.

Counter:

\[
1
\]

Player A successfully completes the next turn.

Counter:

\[
0
\]

No removal occurs.

\subsection*{Example 2}

Player B times out three consecutive turns.

Counter progression:

\[
1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3
\]

Player B is automatically removed from the current match.

% =========================================================
\section{Effect on Gameplay}

Removing inactive players allows the remaining participants to continue without unnecessary interruption.

The software automatically maintains:

\begin{itemize}
    \item valid turn order;
    \item synchronized multiplayer state;
    \item official scores;
    \item sentence history.
\end{itemize}

No previously accepted gameplay is discarded when a player is removed.

% =========================================================
\section{Multiplayer Continuity}

Automatic player removal supports the principle of Computational Facilitation.

The software coordinates participation while leaving interpretation, creativity, and social interaction entirely to the players.

This rule exists to ensure that inactive participants do not indefinitely prevent an otherwise active match from reaching its natural conclusion.

% =========================================================
\section{Official Match Completion}

Every official match concludes through one of two pathways:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Standard Match Completion}, which represents the normal method of determining a winner; or
    \item \textbf{Exceptional Match Completion}, which preserves multiplayer integrity when normal gameplay can no longer continue.
\end{enumerate}

Standard Match Completion always takes precedence whenever it remains possible.

Exceptional Match Completion exists solely to ensure that abandoned or inactive matches reach a well-defined conclusion.

% =========================================================
\section{Standard Match Completion}

The normal progression of every match is illustrated below.

\begin{center}
Normal Gameplay

$\downarrow$

First Player Reaches Target Score

$\downarrow$

Final Round

$\downarrow$

Winner Determination

$\downarrow$

If Leaders are Tied

$\downarrow$

Sudden Death

$\downarrow$

Winner
\end{center}

This sequence represents the official method for determining the winner of The Last Word War\texttrademark.

% =========================================================
\section{Target Score}

Unless otherwise specified by the selected game mode, the official target score is:

\begin{center}
\Large\textbf{10 Points}
\end{center}

The first player to reach or exceed the target score does \textbf{not} immediately win the match.

Instead, the match enters the Final Round.

% =========================================================
\section{Final Round}

The Final Round exists to ensure fairness by providing every active participant with an equal number of turn opportunities.

Immediately after a player reaches the target score:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the match enters the Final Round;
    \item every remaining active player receives exactly one final turn;
    \item no additional players may join the match.
\end{itemize}

Only after every remaining active player has completed that final turn may the winner be determined.

\subsection{Example}

Suppose the current scores are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Victor & 10 \\
Sarah & 8 \\
James & 7 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Victor reaches the target score first.

The match does \textbf{not} end immediately.

Sarah and James each receive one final turn before the winner is determined.

% =========================================================
\section{Winner Determination}

After the Final Round has concluded, the player with the highest official score is declared the winner.

Example:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Victor & 10 \\
Sarah & 9 \\
James & 8 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Victor wins the match.

Once declared, the official winner cannot be changed by any later analysis or review.

% =========================================================
\section{Tie After the Final Round}

If two or more players remain tied for the highest score after the Final Round, the match proceeds to Sudden Death.

Only the tied players continue participating.

All other players have completed the match and take no further turns.

Example:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Victor & 10 \\
Sarah & 10 \\
James & 8 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Victor and Sarah continue.

James leaves the active competition.

% =========================================================
\section{Sudden Death}

Sudden Death exists solely to resolve ties among the highest-scoring players.

Gameplay continues using the normal turn sequence, but only among the tied participants.

All standard gameplay rules remain in effect, including:

\begin{itemize}
    \item sentence construction;
    \item countdown timing;
    \item timeout penalties;
    \item consecutive timeout tracking;
    \item automatic removal for inactivity.
\end{itemize}

% =========================================================
\section{Winning Sudden Death}

A Sudden Death participant wins immediately upon establishing a lead of at least:

\begin{center}
\Large\textbf{Two Points}
\end{center}

Example:

\[
10-10
\]

\[
11-10
\]

\[
11-11
\]

\[
12-11
\]

\[
13-11
\]

The player leading:

\[
13-11
\]

wins the match.

% =========================================================
\section{Maximum Sudden Death Duration}

To prevent matches from continuing indefinitely, Sudden Death is limited to:

\begin{center}
\Large\textbf{Five Complete Sudden Death Rounds}
\end{center}

A Sudden Death Round is completed when every remaining Sudden Death participant has received one turn.

If, after five complete Sudden Death rounds, no player has achieved the required two-point advantage, the match immediately concludes as an official draw.

Example:

\begin{center}
Victor --- 15

Sarah --- 15
\end{center}

After five complete Sudden Death rounds:

\begin{center}
Victor --- 20

Sarah --- 20
\end{center}

Result:

\begin{center}
\Large\textbf{Official Draw}
\end{center}

Neither player is declared the winner.

Both players are officially recorded as tied.

This draw condition represents the normal conclusion of an unresolved Sudden Death and is considered part of the Standard Match Completion process.

% =========================================================
\section{Exceptional Match Completion}

Although every match is intended to conclude through the standard victory procedure, circumstances may arise in which normal gameplay can no longer continue.

To preserve multiplayer integrity, the game defines exceptional match completion rules.

These rules exist only when standard gameplay cannot reasonably continue.

They never replace the normal victory conditions.

% =========================================================
\subsection{Single Active Player Remaining}

If, during Active Play, the Final Round, or Sudden Death, automatic inactivity removal leaves only one active player remaining, the match immediately concludes.

That remaining player is declared the official winner.

Example:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Player A & Active\\
Player B & Removed (Inactivity)\\
Player C & Removed (Inactivity)\\
Player D & Removed (Inactivity)\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Result:

\begin{center}
\Large\textbf{Player A Wins}
\end{center}

No Final Round or Sudden Death is required because normal multiplayer competition can no longer continue.

% =========================================================
\subsection{No Active Players Remaining}

If every remaining participant is removed because of inactivity, the match immediately ends.

Result:

\begin{center}
\Large\textbf{Match Abandoned Due to Inactivity}
\end{center}

No winner is declared.

The official match record shall indicate that the match was abandoned before completion.

% =========================================================
\subsection{Priority of Standard Victory}

Whenever standard gameplay can still continue, the standard victory procedure always takes precedence.

Exceptional Match Completion is evaluated only when normal gameplay has become impossible.

The priority order is therefore:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Standard Match Completion
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Normal Gameplay
        \item Final Round
        \item Winner Determination
        \item Sudden Death (if required)
        \item Official Draw (if Sudden Death reaches five complete rounds)
    \end{itemize}

    \item Exceptional Match Completion
    \begin{itemize}
        \item One Active Player Remaining
        \item No Active Players Remaining
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Rematches}

After a match concludes, players may immediately begin another match without creating a new room.

A rematch preserves the player roster while resetting gameplay.

The following are reset:

\begin{itemize}
    \item scores;
    \item sentence history;
    \item current word;
    \item timer;
    \item timeout counters;
    \item match state;
    \item winner status.
\end{itemize}

The following remain unchanged:

\begin{itemize}
    \item player names;
    \item room membership;
    \item player identities.
\end{itemize}

% =========================================================
\subsection{Starter Rotation}

To eliminate any long-term first-player advantage, the starting player rotates after every completed match.

Example for four players:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Match & Starting Player\\
\midrule
1 & Player A\\
2 & Player B\\
3 & Player C\\
4 & Player D\\
5 & Player A\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Starter rotation applies regardless of how the previous match concluded.

\section{Session Recovery}

Temporary network interruptions should not unnecessarily end a match.

Where supported by the platform, players may reconnect to an ongoing match using their existing player identity.

Session recovery preserves:

\begin{itemize}
    \item player identity;
    \item room membership;
    \item official scores;
    \item sentence history;
    \item current match state.
\end{itemize}

Session recovery exists to maintain continuity rather than provide competitive advantage.

\section{Optional AI Gameplay Analysis}

The Last Word War\texttrademark\ intentionally distinguishes between two separate activities:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Gameplay
    \item Reflection
\end{enumerate}

Gameplay belongs entirely to the participating players.

Reflection may optionally involve artificial intelligence.

Consequently, Artificial Intelligence is \textbf{not} part of official gameplay.

It performs no refereeing role and has no authority over the official outcome of any match.

% =========================================================
\subsection{Availability}

After a match has officially concluded, players may request an optional AI Gameplay Analysis.

Requesting an analysis is entirely voluntary.

Choosing not to perform an analysis has absolutely no effect on the completed match.

% =========================================================
\subsection{Official Match Principle}

Once a match has officially concluded:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the official winner cannot change;
    \item the official scoreboard cannot change;
    \item the official sentence history cannot change;
    \item the official match record cannot change.
\end{itemize}

The AI Gameplay Analysis is completely independent of the official match record.

% =========================================================
\subsection{Purpose}

The purpose of AI Gameplay Analysis is educational reflection.

It may help players better understand:

\begin{itemize}
    \item grammar;
    \item sentence continuity;
    \item factual accuracy;
    \item logical consistency;
    \item scientific correctness;
    \item language usage;
    \item communication quality.
\end{itemize}

The objective is to promote discussion and learning rather than to judge the official gameplay.

% =========================================================
\subsection{Possible Report Contents}

Depending on the available analysis services, an AI Gameplay Analysis report may include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item official match summary;
    \item sentence-by-sentence observations;
    \item grammatical suggestions;
    \item continuity observations;
    \item factual references;
    \item educational recommendations;
    \item gameplay statistics;
    \item communication insights.
\end{itemize}

These observations are informative only.

They never replace the official match record.

% =========================================================
\subsection{Human Authority}

Players remain free to:

\begin{itemize}
    \item agree with the analysis;
    \item disagree with the analysis;
    \item discuss the analysis;
    \item ignore the analysis completely.
\end{itemize}

Artificial intelligence provides recommendations rather than authoritative judgments.

Human interpretation remains the highest authority within The Last Word War\texttrademark.

\section{Frequently Asked Questions}

\subsection*{Does the software judge whether my sentence is good?}

No.

Players remain responsible for interpreting sentence quality and acceptable gameplay.

\subsection*{Can the software decide that my sentence is grammatically incorrect?}

Not during gameplay.

Optional AI Gameplay Analysis may provide educational observations after the match has ended.

\subsection*{Can players create their own house rules?}

Yes.

Provided every participant agrees before or during the match, players may adopt additional local rules that do not conflict with the official structure of gameplay.

\subsection*{Can new players join after the match has started?}

No.

Once Active Play begins, the match is closed to new participants.

\subsection*{What happens if someone repeatedly stops playing?}

A player who accumulates three consecutive timeout penalties is automatically removed from the current match.

This allows the remaining participants to continue playing.

\subsection*{Can the AI change the winner after the match?}

No.

The official winner, official scores, and official match history are permanent once the match concludes.
